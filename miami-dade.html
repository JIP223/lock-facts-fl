Locksmiths in Miami-Dade: what you can verify after county licenses ended

Licensing, verified. Florida does not issue a statewide locksmith license. Miami-Dade County previously licensed locksmiths, but that local occupational license has ended. On July 8, 2025, the county Department of Regulatory and Economic Resources published a notice that, effective July 1, it “is no longer issuing licenses” for “Locksmiths and locksmith services businesses,” because the county “no longer has regulatory authority over these occupations,” citing Florida Statutes § 163.211. The county’s Consumer Protection home page repeats the July 1, 2025 preemption. § 163.211 says occupational licensing is preempted to the state, and that local occupational licenses of the kind described “expire on July 1, 2025,” unless a separate general law authorizes them. There is no replacement state locksmith license.

Official sources we opened

  • Miami-Dade County, “State preempts several business licenses issued by RER,” July 8, 2025
  • Miami-Dade County, Consumer Protection home page (important message on the July 1, 2025 preemption). Locksmiths are not in the remaining “Consumer Protection Business Licenses” list on that page.
  • Florida Statutes § 163.211 (2026), Licensing of occupations preempted to state

An older county page titled “Locksmith Services” still describes registration and identification cards. Given the July 2025 notice and the Consumer Protection home page, we treat that older language as stale and do not rely on it.

A Local Business Tax Receipt (a tax on doing business, not an occupational locksmith license) may still be required by the county or a city. We did not locate a complete public locksmith-specific LBT directory to scrape for this first page, so we are not listing tax-receipt numbers.

What you can still check yourself

  • Look up the exact legal name on search.sunbiz.org. Confirm it is Active, and compare the principal address to a physical shop — not only a Google listing.
  • Ask for a firm, written total before anyone starts work. A trip charge is not a total.
  • Ask whether they have a storefront you can see on Street View, and whether they will take a credit card (so you have a dispute path).
  • Voluntary trade memberships (for example ALOA) are not government licenses.
  • If work includes alarm-system installation, that can fall under a different Florida contractor license. This page does not cover alarm contracting.

The $19 bait-and-switch, in plain terms

A pattern that shows up again and again in consumer complaints: you search “locksmith near me,” see a very low price (often advertised around $19–$29, sometimes quoted a bit higher by phone), and someone arrives quickly. The on-site bill is then several hundred dollars, sometimes with pressure to pay cash. The low number was a hook, not the total.

  • Ask for the legal business name and a firm total for your exact situation (car lockout, apartment door, rekey) before anyone is dispatched or starts work.
  • If they will only quote a “service call” and say the technician will set the price on site, that is a warning sign — not proof of a crime, but a reason to call someone else.
  • A $19–$29 “visit” price that is not a complete job price is the usual bait.
  • Prefer paying with a credit card, and get a written invoice with the legal name on it.
  • An Active Sunbiz record is helpful and still not a substitute for a written total.
